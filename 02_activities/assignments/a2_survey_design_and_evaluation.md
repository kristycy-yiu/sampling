## Part B - Survey Evaluation:

Identify and describe survey features:

1. Sample type: stratified design at the census metropolitan area level using probability sampling

2. Sample size: For each province, minimum sample sizes were determined that would ensure certain estimates would have acceptable sampling variability at the stratum level. Once these stratum sample size targets had been met, the remaining sample was allocated to the strata in a way that balanced the need for precision of both national-level and stratum-level estimates. 

3. Target population: all persons 15 years of age and older living in the ten provinces of Canada

4. Sampling frame: combination of landline and cellular telephone numbers from the Census and various administrative sources with Statistics Canada's dwelling frame 

5. Survey mode(s): electronic questionnaire or computer assisted telephone interviewing

6. Timeline: 2018-09-04 to 2018-12-28 (data collection for this reference period)

7. Response rate: 41.9%

8. Weights: To adjust for the 'rejecting' of a proportion of respondents that are not volunteers, the person weight for respondents that are not 'rejected' and are not volunteers is multiplied by a factor. The weights were adjusted so that the weighted income distribution of GVP matched the 2017 CIS distribution by province.

9. Data processing: Information collected were linked to personal tax records for purposes of obtaining better quality data. The SSPE set of generalized processing was used in error detection. It used a structured environment to monitor the processing of data ensuring best practices and harmonized business processes were followed. The CATI data capture program allowed a valid range of codes for each question and built-in edits, and automatically follows the flow of the questionnaire. The computer edits occurred concurrently while the interview was conducted.

10. Cleaning, imputation, etc: Most missing data, with exception of a few cases, were filled using donor imputation, where each incomplete record (recipient) was matched to similar complete records (donors) based on shared characteristics. A score function determined similarity — donors received higher scores for each matching characteristic, and the highest-scoring donor’s data were used to fill in missing values. If multiple donors were tied, one was randomly chosen. Donor pools were constructed to ensure that imputed values were consistent with other data on the recipient record. When donor imputation wasn’t possible, mean imputation was applied.
Imputation occurred in nine steps: first for personal and family income, followed by formal volunteering variables, then informal volunteering variables, and lastly, donation and solicitation variables.

11. Sources of error: There are three sources of error: representation, measurement, and processing. 
Representation error includes coverage, sampling, and nonresponse. 
Coverage error occurs when the frame population is different from the target population. 
Sampling error occurs the sample population is different from the frame population. This is a variance (random error).
Non-response error is introduced when the respondents do not answer questions. To correct for this error for questions on household income, additional adjustment was conducted by using information extracted from administrative sources (e.g., income and household composition) to model and adjust for nonresponse.
Measurement error occurs when the survey responses are different from the true population value. This may occur due to: the respondent being untruthful; misunderstanding of the question; respondent forgetting the truth; social likeability; the order the question was presented.
Processing error can occur during data entry or editing and sources include incorrectly transcribing the telephone interviews; typos when entering data; or coding multiple responses as a single response in open-ended questions (there are some open-ended questions in the questionnaire).

12. Limitations, known biases, etc: Selection bias occurs when some population units are excluded from the sample population. In this survey, it may occur due to coverage error as households without telephones and those with telephone services not covered by the frame, are excluded from the surveyed population.
Coverage bias occurs when coverage error causes sample estimates to differ from the population value. Given there is known coverage error in this study, coverage bias is a possibility.
Measurement bias occurs when measurement errors occur consistently in one direction. Given that measurement error may be present in this study, measurement bias is also a possibility.
